# Evidence base for training-diary decisions

Reviewed **2026-09-07**. Source IDs and access status are defined in
[README.md](./README.md). These rules supplement `ATHLETE.md`; they do not
replace current athlete data, subjective feedback, or medical judgment.

## How to use this evidence

1. Start with the athlete's current goal, phase, recent diary trend, and fresh
   recovery/training data.
2. Use the smallest relevant evidence set, normally zero to three sources per
   diary decision. Do not add citations that do not change the recommendation.
3. Separate direct evidence from extrapolation. Population, sport, sex,
   training status, event duration, and study design all matter.
4. Prefer converging signals over a single score. Escalate uncertainty when
   objective and subjective signals disagree.
5. Historical diary patterns are context, not current state. The current local
   archive ends on **2026-07-24**; fetch fresh data before prescribing the final
   Ciclobrava taper or assessing present readiness.

## Load, intensity, and taper

- Treat TSS and the Performance Manager model as descriptive load context.
  CTL is a roughly 42-day exponentially weighted load, ATL roughly 7-day load,
  and TSB their difference. Missing rides, stale FTP, non-cycling load, and
  individual response can all distort the picture. Never infer readiness from
  TSB alone. **Sources:** S01, S07.
- Count session intent as well as minutes in zones. As a practical guardrail,
  keep most endurance sessions genuinely easy and avoid turning group rides
  into unplanned threshold days. The often-cited approximately 75-80% low,
  5-10% threshold, and 15-20% high distribution is observational and should not
  be enforced as a daily quota. **Source:** S04.
- Periodization is a feedback framework: sequence emphases, then revise from
  observed response. Do not impose a rigid block model when the athlete's
  schedule, recovery, or event specificity argues otherwise. **Source:** S05.
- For an endurance-event taper, preserve some intensity and frequency while
  reducing accumulated volume. The strongest general starting point is an
  approximately two-week progressive taper with roughly 40-60% less volume
  than the athlete's recent normal load. Individual response and the actual
  pre-taper workload override a formula. Do not chase weight loss during race
  week. **Sources:** S02, S03, S13.

## Recovery, HRV, and resting heart rate

- Interpret morning HRV longitudinally against the athlete's own baseline and
  measurement noise. A single high or low reading is not a diagnosis; even the
  direction of adaptation can differ between elite endurance athletes.
  **Sources:** S06, S07.
- Standardize measurement conditions and prefer simple resting HR plus rMSSD
  trends over speculative interpretation of spectral ratios. **Source:** S07.
- HRV reflects cardiac autonomic state, not glycogen status, muscle damage,
  soreness, motivation, or total readiness. Combine it with sleep duration and
  continuity, resting HR, recent load, soreness, illness symptoms, subjective
  freshness, and actual performance. **Source:** S07.
- Preserve the repo's athlete-specific interpretation: low Garmin Training
  Readiness with BALANCED HRV usually indicates accumulated load rather than
  autonomic failure. Still cross-check sleep, soreness, nutrition, and recent
  performance before prescribing work.

## Sleep

- For adults, 7-9 hours is a useful population reference, not an individualized
  pass/fail threshold. Athletes can require more, especially during heavy load
  and before competition. **Sources:** S08, S09.
- Prioritize duration, sleep opportunity, latency, awakenings, wake after sleep
  onset, and sleep efficiency over wearable-estimated REM/deep-stage minutes.
  Consumer devices are useful for trends but can misclassify wake and stages.
  **Sources:** S08, S10.
- Useful adult continuity anchors are sleep latency no more than about 30
  minutes, no more than one awakening longer than five minutes, wake after
  sleep onset around 20 minutes or less, and sleep efficiency at least 85%.
  These are screening anchors, not medical diagnoses. **Source:** S10.
- In the days before a key event, create extra sleep opportunity rather than
  trying to repair a deficit on the final night. A short early-afternoon nap
  can help when needed; avoid letting sleep tracking create anxiety. **Source:**
  S08.

## Carbohydrate and hydration

- Match carbohydrate intake to event duration, absolute intensity, gut
  tolerance, and opportunity to eat. Use absolute grams per hour rather than
  scaling on body mass during exercise. **Sources:** S11, S12.
- Operational ranges:
  - under about 75 minutes: water as needed; a carbohydrate mouth rinse or
    small amount can help during hard work;
  - 1-2 hours: around 30 g/h, rising toward 60 g/h when intensity or duration
    demands it;
  - 2-3 hours: around 60 g/h;
  - over 2.5-3 hours: up to 90 g/h using multiple transportable carbohydrates
    if practiced and tolerated.
  **Sources:** S11, S12, S15.
- For this athlete, retain **60-80 g/h for rides at least 90 minutes** and
  **70-90 g/h for 4-5+ hour rides/events**. The repeated diary failure mode is
  delayed or insufficient intake, so execution and timing matter more than
  raising the ceiling.
- Prefer a glucose/maltodextrin plus fructose mixture near the upper end. It can
  improve exogenous carbohydrate delivery and gut comfort versus glucose alone,
  but individual tolerance still governs. **Source:** S15.
- Do not prescribe 120 g/h as the Ciclobrava default. The 120 g/h trial involved
  20 elite male mountain runners with prior gut training and measured markers
  of exercise-induced muscle damage, not cycling performance in this athlete.
  Treat 120 g/h only as a future, progressively trained experiment well away
  from a key event. **Source:** S14.
- Practice the event plan in training. Coordinate carbohydrate concentration,
  solids, fluid, heat, and sodium because a dense fueling plan can compromise
  fluid delivery or GI tolerance. Preserve the athlete's existing hydration
  floor of at least 600 ml/h, adjusted for conditions and measured response.
  **Sources:** S11, S12.
- Before long competition, favor high carbohydrate availability; do not use a
  key event as a train-low session. When rapid recovery is required, target
  about 1.0-1.2 g carbohydrate/kg/h for the first four hours plus roughly
  0.25-0.3 g protein/kg early in recovery. **Sources:** S12, S13.

## Energy availability and body mass

- Pursue body-composition change well before competition, with a modest energy
  deficit and high protein. Slow loss is more compatible with preserving lean
  mass and training quality than an aggressive late cut. **Source:** S13.
- In taper and race week, maintenance energy and carbohydrate availability take
  priority over the scale. The athlete-specific rule remains: lighter and
  durable, not empty and fragile.

## Caffeine

- Caffeine is optional, not a recovery substitute. Endurance performance has
  the most consistent benefit, but response and side effects vary. **Source:**
  S16.
- Evidence is strongest around 3-6 mg/kg approximately 60 minutes before
  exercise, while benefits may begin near 2 mg/kg and 9 mg/kg adds side effects
  without a clear need. For this athlete, begin only with a previously tolerated
  low dose around **2-3 mg/kg**; never introduce or escalate it on event day.
- Consider timing later in a long event only if rehearsed. Protect sleep:
  caffeine's typical half-life is roughly 4-6 hours and late intake can worsen
  latency, duration, and sleep efficiency. **Source:** S16.

## Diary decision checklist

Before writing the coach note, answer:

1. Is the recommendation based on current data or on stale historical context?
2. Are load, autonomic, sleep, muscular, fueling, and subjective signals aligned?
3. Is today's session intent still compatible with the active phase and event?
4. Does tomorrow's carbohydrate target match the actual duration/intensity?
5. Is any numerical claim extrapolated beyond the source population?
6. Which zero to three source IDs materially support the decision? If sources
   change the recommendation, add a short `Evidence basis:` line to the diary.
